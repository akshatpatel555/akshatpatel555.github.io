Stitching Client Manager

A tablet-first Flutter app that replaces a paper notebook with searchable customer records, structured work orders, stylus handwriting, and per-order payment tracking for a custom tailoring business.

Problem

My mom runs a stitching and alteration side business — designing and altering Indian dresses, blouses, lehengas, and more for clients. She tracked everything in a paper notebook: customer names, what work needed to be done, measurements, due dates, and whether orders were paid. There was no way to search for a customer, no structured way to specify garment types or work types (alteration, new borders, custom design), and no reliable tracking of completion or payment status across multiple concurrent orders.

Solution

I built a tablet-first Android app designed around how she actually works. Each customer has a profile with contact info and reusable measurement sets per garment type. Orders capture garment type, multiple work tags, typed notes, S Pen handwriting, photos (fabric, design references, finished pieces), due dates, and payment status — deposits, partial payments, and paid-in-full tracking. A dashboard with search and filters (unpaid, overdue, in progress, due this week) gives her an at-a-glance view of what needs attention.

Architecture

  • Flutter UI with Provider for dependency injection and a repository pattern separating data access from screens
  • Drift ORM over local SQLite — reactive streams power live-updating lists as orders change
  • Normalized schema: customers, orders, garment/work tags, measurements, handwriting strokes, and categorized photos with cascade deletes
  • Configurable tag system — default garment types (blouse, lehenga, kurti) and work types (alteration, embroidery, hemming) with custom additions
  • Scribble-based handwriting canvas with serialized stroke data and fullscreen editing mode for stylus input
  • Speech-to-text integration for hands-free note dictation while measuring or fitting
  • Structured for future iPad port and optional cloud sync without schema changes

Design Tradeoffs

  • Local-first vs. cloud — chose SQLite on-device so the app works offline with zero setup and no subscription costs for a small business. Cloud sync is architected as a future layer, not a v1 requirement.
  • Tablet-first vs. phone — optimized layouts for Samsung tablet + S Pen workflow rather than a one-size-fits-all responsive design, since that's the primary use case.
  • Flexible tags vs. rigid forms — configurable garment and work tags let her describe orders in her own vocabulary instead of forcing predefined dropdowns that don't match how Indian tailoring work is described.

Results

  • v1.3 deployed and in daily use — customer lookup, order creation, and payment tracking fully replaced the paper notebook
  • Dashboard filters surface overdue and unpaid orders immediately instead of flipping through pages
  • Reusable customer measurement sets eliminate re-entering measurements for repeat clients
  • Unit tests cover core business logic (balance due, overdue detection, payment status)
